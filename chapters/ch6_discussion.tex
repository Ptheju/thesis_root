\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Summary}
This dissertation investigated PCA-derived channel selection for multivariate time-series classification. It distinguished conventional latent-space projection from reduction of the physical channel axis, formulated alternative loading-based scores, and evaluated selection using a training-only inner-validation protocol followed by held-out testing and an empirical random-subset null.

The principal empirical finding is that substantial spatial compression can retain competitive predictive performance. FingerMovements was reduced from 28 to seven channels and FaceDetection from 144 to 36. Selected-subset accuracy exceeded the corresponding full-array accuracy in both supplied primary results. However, the evidence for superiority over random subsets was dataset-dependent and did not reach $p<0.05$. These findings support cautious claims about performance retention and sensor economy, not a general claim that PCA selection identifies statistically superior channels.

\section{Contributions}
The work contributes: (i) a comparison of multiple PCA-derived channel-selection criteria; (ii) an explicit distinction between unsupervised loading methods and supervised class-conditional subspace divergence; (iii) a leakage-resistant selection and evaluation protocol; and (iv) a critical empirical comparison with random channel subsets rather than only a full-array baseline.

\section{Limitations}
The evaluation covers few datasets, with a ceiling effect on BasicMotions and heterogeneous signal characteristics across the remaining benchmarks. The Monte Carlo analysis uses only 100 draws, limiting precision. Accuracy alone may conceal class-specific errors. PCA models only linear covariance structure, and results may depend on scaling, classifier configuration and random seed. The supervised subspace method also requires labels and cannot be interpreted as wholly unsupervised dimensionality reduction. The supplied performance profile is synthetic and consists of a single reported run, so it does not establish general runtime or memory savings. Finally, a selected EEG index should not be given a neurophysiological interpretation until the channel-to-electrode mapping is verified.

\section{Future work}
Future work should increase the number of random draws, evaluate additional datasets, repeat experiments across seeds, and report uncertainty for both selected and baseline performance. Comparisons with established supervised sensor-selection, sparse PCA, dynamic PCA and nonlinear approaches would clarify the method's relative value. Robustness to missing or noisy sensors and validation on independent cohorts would strengthen practical claims. Where sensor economy is a goal, runtime, memory and acquisition-cost reductions should be measured directly.

\section{Final conclusion}
PCA-derived selection can remove a large fraction of channels while retaining downstream performance, but the benefit beyond cardinality-matched random selection is not consistent. The most defensible contribution is therefore the combination of interpretable spatial reduction and rigorous evaluation, together with a transparent account of where the empirical evidence is strong, weak or incomplete.
