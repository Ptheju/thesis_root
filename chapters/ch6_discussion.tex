\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\noindent
This chapter synthesizes the empirical findings of this investigation, contextualizing the outcomes of spatial dimensionality reduction across multivariate time series (MTS) benchmarks. By examining the interplay between unsupervised variance preservation, supervised subspace divergence, and non-parametric null testing, this section interprets the broader implications of the study for time series analytics and sensor economy.

\section{Interpretation of Empirical Findings}
\noindent
The dataset dependence observed here is consistent with prior evidence that
multivariate TSC performance varies materially across representations and
domains \citep{ruiz2021multivariate,middlehurst2024bakeoff}.The primary empirical takeaway of this dissertation is that substantial spatial dimensionality reduction—pruning up to 75\% of physical sensor arrays—can be achieved without degrading downstream classification accuracy. However, the efficacy of the underlying selection rules is heavily bounded by domain topology and signal characteristics. 

For the sensorimotor \textit{FingerMovements} EEG dataset ($C = 28$), unsupervised maximum loading (\texttt{PCA-Max Loading}) successfully identified a 7-channel subset that matched or slightly exceeded full-array performance while discarding three-quarters of the electrode montage. Conversely, for the high-density visual ERP paradigm (\textit{FaceDetection}, $C = 144$), unsupervised variance maximization proved insufficient; optimal performance required class-conditional subspace rotation divergence, yielding 64.02\% accuracy ($k = 36$) compared to the 62.77\% full-array baseline. 

This divergence underscores a fundamental methodological insight: unsupervised variance-based PCA excels when dominant orthogonal axes capture structured kinematic or oscillatory energy (as in motion capture and motor rhythms), whereas high-density neuroimaging plagued by distributed background noise necessitates supervised manifold comparisons to isolate task-discriminative cortical dynamics.

\section{Statistical Rigor Versus Conventional Significance}
\noindent
Effect magnitude and $p$-value evidence address different questions and
should be reported together
\citep{lakens2013effect,wasserstein2016pvalue}.A defining methodological contribution of this work is the integration of an empirical Monte Carlo permutation testing harness ($R = 100$). Rather than relying solely on full-array accuracy comparisons, evaluating selected subsets against cardinality-grained random distributions revealed crucial nuances regarding statistical significance.

While \textit{FaceDetection} achieved a large standardized effect size ($d = 1.47$) relative to its narrow null spread, the pseudo-count corrected empirical $p$-value ($p = 0.0792$) remained above the conventional $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold. This outcome demonstrates that standardized separation can highlight meaningful practical utility even when finite permutation bounds prevent strict null rejection. It emphasizes why researchers must report effect magnitudes alongside $p$-values rather than treating significance thresholds as binary indicators of success.

\section{Methodological Trade-Offs and Limitations}
\noindent
Robust covariance estimators \citep{ledoit2004covariance} and nonlinear
extensions such as kernel PCA \citep{scholkopf1998kernelpca} provide natural
comparisons for these limitations. Despite the positive compression results, several limitations must be acknowledged:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Covariance Sensitivity:} Empirical covariance matrices remain highly vulnerable to correlated environmental noise, ocular artifacts, and transient high-amplitude spikes. Because PCA maximizes variance linearly, high-energy artifacts can dominate principal loading vectors independently of class relevance.
    \item \textbf{Monte Carlo Resolution:} The use of $R = 100$ permutation draws provides a reliable empirical null distribution but limits precise tail-probability resolution. Increasing $R$ in future implementations would refine empirical $p$-value bounds.
    \item \textbf{Linearity Constraints:} Standard PCA captures linear second-order dependencies. Complex nonlinear cross-channel interactions in high-density EEG may require manifold learning or kernelized alternatives.
\end{itemize}
