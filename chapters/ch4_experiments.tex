\chapter{Experimental Design and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{chap:experimental_design}

\noindent
Rigorous evaluation of spatial dimensionality reduction in multivariate time series (MTS) requires a standardized, reproducible experimental protocol. Because feature and channel selection introduce structural transformations prior to classification, flawed evaluation designs can easily lead to data leakage, selection bias, and overoptimistic generalization estimates. This chapter details the experimental architecture designed for this study, covering dataset specifications, data hygiene protocols, feature transformation pipelines, nested cross-validation frameworks, and non-parametric statistical validation harnesses.

\section{Benchmark Datasets and Topologies}
\label{sec:datasets}

\noindent
All three benchmarks come from the UEA multivariate time-series archive
\citep{bagnall2018uea}.To evaluate the generalizability of the spatial channel selection framework across diverse recording domains, three distinct benchmark datasets were selected from the time series classification repository:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{BasicMotions ($C = 6$):} A low-dimensional motion capture dataset comprising 6 inertial sensor channels (accelerometer and gyroscope axes) recording simple physical movements. It serves as a baseline for low-complexity multi-axis temporal synchronization.
    \item \textbf{FingerMovements ($C = 28$):} A sensorimotor electroencephalography (EEG) dataset consisting of 28 channels capturing neural oscillations ($\mu/\beta$ rhythms) associated with motor tasks.
    \item \textbf{FaceDetection ($C = 144$):} A high-density neuroimaging dataset comprising 144 sensor channels recording visual event-related potentials (ERPs) during face and object recognition trials.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Each dataset provides distinct spatial topologies, varying from tightly coupled orthogonal motion axes to spatially smooth, high-density cortical scalp potentials.

\section{Data Hygiene and Leakage Prevention Protocols}
\label{sec:data_hygiene}

\noindent
The split design follows guidance on avoiding selection bias and leakage
\citep{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting,kaufman2012leakage,
krstajic2014crossvalidation}.A primary methodological risk in automated channel selection is inadvertent data leakage, where information from the evaluation test split contaminates the feature selection or training phase. To ensure absolute academic validity, a strict data hygiene protocol is enforced across all experiments:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Partition Isolation:} All spatial covariance calculations, eigenvalue decompositions, PCA loading extractions, subspace rotation divergences, and ANOVA $F$-score rankings are computed \textbf{strictly and exclusively on the training partition} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}, \mathbf{y}_{\text{train}}$).
    \item \textbf{Sequestration of Test Data:} The test partition ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}, \mathbf{y}_{\text{test}}$) remains completely unseen and untouched during the entire channel identification and subset selection phase.
    \item \textbf{Independent Spatial Slicing:} Once the optimal sparse subset mapping $\mathcal{S}^*$ of size $k^*$ is derived from training statistics, both training and test tensors are independently sliced along the spatial axis:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}^{*} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}[:, \mathcal{S}^^*, :], \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}^{*} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}[:, \mathcal{S}^^*, :]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Robust Preprocessing and Scaling:} To mitigate extreme outlier distortion across sensor channels, input values undergo robust min-max scaling where scaling boundaries are determined exclusively via the \textbf{98th percentile} of the training distribution prior to tensor reshaping and zero-mean column-wise centering.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Feature Extraction and Classification Engine}
\label{sec:classifier_engine}

\noindent
MiniRocket \citep{dempster2021minirocket} and ridge classification
\citep{hoerl1970ridge,pedregosa2011sklearn} provide the common evaluation
engine.To isolate the impact of spatial channel reduction from classifier-specific artifacts, a standardized, high-performance downstream classification pipeline is maintained across all evaluations utilizing established machine learning libraries (\texttt{aeon} version 0.4.x and \texttt{scikit-learn}):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feature Transformation (\texttt{MiniRocket}):} Rather than operating directly on raw multivariate tensors or handcrafted statistical descriptors, reduced tensors ($\mathbf{X}^* \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times k^* \times T}$) are processed via \texttt{MiniRocket}. This architecture applies 10,000 randomly initialized convolutional kernels of length 9 to efficiently capture temporal phase and shape characteristics, projecting MTS data into a scalable feature matrix. A fixed random seed ($\texttt{random\_state} = 42$) is enforced across all transformer initializations.
    \item \textbf{Classification Estimator (\texttt{RidgeClassifierCV}):} Downstream classification is executed using a ridge regression classifier with built-in generalized cross-validation, automatically optimizing regularization parameters across a logarithmic scale ($\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]$) without manual hyperparameter tuning on the test set.
\end{itemize}

\section{Nested Cross-Validatory Setup and Hyperparameter Grids}
\label{sec:nested_cv}

\noindent
Nested selection separates configuration choice from final assessment and
limits optimistic bias
\citep{varma2006bias,cawley2010overfitting}.To determine optimal subset sizes ($k^*$) and selection methods without overfitting, a nested evaluation structure is implemented:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inner Cross-Validation:} Five-fold cross-validation is executed entirely within the training partition to evaluate candidate subset sizes and selection criteria. For instance, grid sweeps evaluated candidate grids such as $k \in [2, 3]$ for \textit{BasicMotions}, $k \in [3, 4, 7, 14]$ for \textit{FingerMovements}, and $k \in [15, 21, 36, 72]$ for \textit{FaceDetection}.
    \item \textbf{Outer Evaluation:} The configuration maximizing inner cross-validation accuracy is selected and locked before evaluating a single held-out test set. Exploratory grid searches are strictly segregated from primary inferential results to prevent selection bias.
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Parametric Monte Carlo Permutation Testing Harness}
\label{sec:monte_carlo}

\noindent
The empirical test and pseudo-count correction follow
\citet{north2002permutation,phipson2010permutation}; effect sizes are
reported following \citet{cohen1988power,lakens2013effect}.To test whether algorithmic channel selection yields performance gains exceeding random spatial coincidence, a non-parametric Monte Carlo permutation testing harness is integrated:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Null Distribution:} For each evaluation dataset, $R = 100$ independent iterations of uniform random channel subsampling of size $k^*$ are executed under identical downstream classification conditions. This generates a non-parametric empirical null accuracy distribution:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{D}_{\text{rand}} = \left\{ A_{\text{rand}}^{(1)}, A_{\text{rand}}^{(2)}, \dots, A_{\text{rand}}^{(R)} \right\}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Significance Testing ($p$-values):} Statistical divergence between selected subset accuracy $A_{\text{sel}}$ and the random null baseline is quantified using pseudo-count corrected empirical $p$-values:
    \begin{equation}
        p = \frac{1 + \sum_{r=1}^R \mathbb{I}\left( A_{\text{rand}}^{(r)} \ge A_{\text{sel}} \right)}{R + 1}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Standardized Effect Sizes (Cohen's $d$):} Directional separation independent of sample size scaling is measured via Cohen's $d$:
    \begin{equation}
        d = \frac{A_{\text{sel}} - \mu_{\text{rand}}}{\sigma_{\text{rand}}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mu_{\text{rand}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{rand}}$ denote the mean and standard deviation of the random null distribution $\mathcal{D}_{\text{rand}}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Performance Metrics and Computational Profiling Setup}
\label{sec:profiling_setup}

\noindent
Evaluation metrics extend beyond standard classification accuracy to ensure comprehensive analysis:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Classification Metrics:} Overall test accuracy, macro-averaged F1 score, and weighted F1 score are computed to account for class distribution properties.
    \item \textbf{Computational Profiling:} To quantify operational efficiency, profiling scripts track transformation latency, fitting latency, and peak memory allocation (measured in megabytes) when processing uncompressed full-array tensors ($\mathbf{X}$) versus reduced subset tensors ($\mathbf{X}^*$).
\end{itemize}
